Blackout Box

The app that works when nothing else does.

Before the storm, Blackout Box uses Gemma 4 through the Gemini API to load your evacuation zone, the nearest places with power, FEMA's steps, Florida's early-refill law, the FDA storage rules for every medication you photograph, and the NWS forecast onto your device. After the storm, with no power and no signal, the same Gemma 4 runs locally through Ollama and keeps answering, in English, Español, and Kreyòl, from what it saved.

Part A

Concept: for judges and the team

The problem in Miami

One line: after a hurricane, the information people need most (am I in an evacuation zone, where is there power, is my insulin still good, what does FEMA want from me, what does this insurance letter say) lives on websites, and the websites are the first thing to disappear when the grid and the cell towers go down.

Outages last days, not hours

FEMA's own record for Miami-Dade shows the pattern: major disaster declarations for Hurricane Milton (DR-4834, Oct 2024) and Hurricane Nicole (DR-4680, Dec 2022), verified on OpenFEMA this morning. Every one of those came with power and connectivity loss. The apps people installed stop working at exactly the moment they matter.

Heat turns a blackout into a medical problem

Verified this morning at 10:20 AM ET: Miami International read 87.8°F with a 97.7°F heat index. Every insulin and GLP-1 label pulled today caps room-temperature storage at 86°F. Without a fridge, a closed apartment passes that line fast. USDA says refrigerated perishables are unsafe after 4 hours without power.

Three languages, one emergency

Miami's emergencies happen in English, Spanish, and Haitian Creole. Official guidance is mostly English PDFs. The people we are building for: a senior on insulin alone in a Little Haiti apartment, a Spanish-speaking family in Zone B who does not know they are in Zone B, a parent with a flooded ground floor and an insurance letter they cannot parse.

The data exists, and it is public

Miami-Dade publishes its hurricane evacuation zones (2,147 polygons, Zones A to E, verified today), its libraries and fire stations, and its shelters (layer is empty until activation). FEMA, NWS, FDA, USDA, and NREL all have free APIs. Nobody has packaged them to survive a blackout.

Before putting Miami-Dade language or population figures in the pitch, pull them from Census ACS and cite them in the README. This brief does not assert any demographic numbers.

The four forces: Water (surge zones, flooding), Air (the storm), Fire (heat with no AC), Land (where to shelter, where the power is). Blackout Box is the one tool that still answers all four when the grid is gone.

Two modes, one model, the same prompts

PREP MODE · ONLINE · BEFORE THE STORM

Gemma 4 through the Gemini API

  • User enters an address or allows location. The app queries Miami-Dade's HurricaneEvacZone_gdb layer and caches the zone letter (verified: downtown 25.77,-80.19 is Zone B).
  • Caches nearest libraries, fire stations, open shelters (empty until activated; honest empty state), and NREL 24-hour EV chargers as "places likely to have power".
  • Caches the latest OpenFEMA declarations for Miami-Dade, FEMA's "what to do after" steps, Fla. Stat. 252.358 text, FDA emergency insulin guidance, and USDA's outage rule.
  • User photographs their medicine cabinet. Gemma 4 reads the labels into strict JSON; openFDA returns each drug's storage text; the validated rule is cached per drug.
  • Caches the NWS hourly forecast and latest observation. Everything is written to IndexedDB and the app shell to Cache Storage.
BLACKOUT MODE · OFFLINE · AFTER THE STORM

Gemma 4 through Ollama on the laptop

  • The PWA loads from the service worker with no network. The server detects no internet and routes every AI call to http://localhost:11434 (gemma4:e4b).
  • Photograph the fridge and the medicine cabinet: the 4-Hour Rule module gives every item a countdown (USDA 4 h / 40°F for food; FDA label days for meds).
  • Photograph a damaged room, a flooded street, or an insurance letter. Gemma 4 describes hazards, extracts deadlines and claim numbers, and says what to do next from FEMA's cached steps.
  • Ask anything in English, Español, or Kreyòl. Answers are grounded only in the cached context; if the answer is not in the cache, the app says so.
  • Same four prompts, same JSON schemas, same banned-phrase filter as online. Only the transport changes.

Rules the product follows

  • Numbers (countdowns, distances, zone letters) come from data plus deterministic code. The model reads photos, answers from the cache, translates, and describes. It never computes a deadline.
  • Every fact shows its source and when it was cached ("Miami-Dade HurricaneEvacZone_gdb, checked Thu 10:40 AM").
  • Never tells anyone to stop or throw out insulin without a replacement (FDA emergency guidance). Frozen insulin is the one "do not use". Food is different: USDA says discard perishables after 4 h above 40°F, and the app says so plainly.
  • Hazard photos (downed wire, standing water near outlets, gas smell, structural damage) get a fixed "stay out, call 911 when you have service" card, not a generated opinion.
  • If the answer is not in the cached context, the app says "I don't have that saved" and suggests what to check when service returns. No guessing about shelters, roads, or curfews.
  • Banned-phrase filter from Cold Chain stays: reject model output matching /no (la )?uses|don't use|do not use|stop taking|throw (it )?away|discard|pa sèvi/i for any medication item whose status is not DO_NOT_USE, and fall back to fixed i18n copy. (Food items are exempt: "discard" is the correct USDA word there.)

Privacy and honesty

  • Photos are processed in memory and never uploaded anywhere except the chosen AI endpoint for that one call; offline they never leave the laptop. No accounts, no analytics.
  • openFDA's disclaimer ("do not rely on openFDA to make decisions regarding medical care") appears on the About screen and in the README.
  • "Places likely to have power" is a hint. Shelters show the honest empty state when the county layer is empty (it was, at 10:35 AM today).
  • Insurance-letter reads are labeled "extracted by AI, verify against the letter". Never estimate payouts or coverage.

Stack

Vite + TypeScript PWA (web/)

One line why: a service worker and IndexedDB are the only way a browser app survives a reload with no network, and Vite builds the shell list for precache in one command.

Node 20 + Express (server/)

One line why: keys stay server-side, every upstream response is cached to disk for DEMO_MODE, and the same process proxies Ollama so the browser never fights CORS.

Ollama (localhost:11434)

One line why: it is already installed, serves Gemma 4 with vision over a plain REST API, and keeps the model loaded between calls with keep_alive.

Front end details

  • Vanilla TS, no framework. Mobile-first CSS using this brief's tokens (dark screen, amber "lit" accent).
  • Camera: <input type="file" accept="image/*" capture="environment">; resize to long edge 1280 px (smaller than Cold Chain's 1568 because Ollama on e4b is slower with big images), JPEG 0.85, base64.
  • Service worker web/public/sw.js: precache the Vite build manifest files + index.html + manifest.webmanifest; cache-first for the shell; network-first with cache fallback for GET /api/cache/*; passthrough for POST /api/ai/*.
  • IndexedDB via the native API (no library) in web/src/db.ts, DB name blackout-box, version 1, stores in the cache model.
  • Online detection: navigator.onLine plus a 2-second GET /api/health probe; the server reports which AI provider is reachable.
  • Countdowns: one setInterval(1000) re-rendering from deadlineAt.
  • Vite dev proxy: /api → http://localhost:8787. Register the SW in dev too (vite-plugin-pwa is optional; a hand-written sw.js copied by Vite from public/ is enough and has no plugin risk).

Back end details

  • Express via tsx, express.json({limit:"12mb"}), native fetch, no SDKs.
  • server/ai/index.ts picks the provider: AI_PROVIDER=auto tries Gemini → Workers AI → Ollama (first that passes a 3 s health check); or force with gemini|workers|ollama.
  • Disk cache cache/<sha1(url+body)>.json; TTL labels 24 h, weather 10 min, places/zone 24 h, FEMA 6 h. DEMO_MODE=1 = cache and fixtures/ only.
  • Engine: skills/blackout-box/scripts/engine.mjs (pure ESM, no I/O) shared by server, web, and skill CLI. It contains the 4-Hour Rule and the Cold Chain verdict.
  • Tests: node --test test/*.test.mjs. No jest/vitest (machine load rule).

1. Gemini API: Gemma 4 (prize path) MODEL LIST VERIFIED KEY 402 THIS MORNING JSON MODE UNVERIFIED

Endpoint
POST https://generativelanguage.googleapis.com/v1beta/models/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it:generateContent (31B: gemma-4-31b-it). Model list source: GET /v1beta/models?pageSize=1000 returned both IDs this morning; Google's page Gemma on the Gemini API lists the same two.
Auth
Header x-goog-api-key: ${GEMINI_API_KEY}. Read from env only. The key at ~/.config/gemini/nanobanana-key returned HTTP 402 "prepayment credits depleted" today; do not assume it works. First task: run the smoke test below and report the status code.
Verified features
Per Google's Gemma page: system instructions (systemInstruction), image input, multi-turn, function calling, Google Search grounding. The generateContent reference describes systemInstruction as "text only".
Image part
The generateContent reference's shell examples use snake_case "inline_data": {"mime_type": "image/jpeg", "data": "<base64>"}. (The REST API also accepts camelCase inlineData/mimeType; snake_case is what the official examples show, so use that.) Inline request total limit 20 MB; MIME types png, jpeg, webp, heic, heif.
JSON mode
Not verified for Gemma. Neither the Gemma-on-Gemini-API page nor the structured-output page mentions Gemma, and the key could not be used to test. Do not depend on responseMimeType. Use the prompt-only JSON contract below plus fence stripping and one retry, which is what the Ollama path uses too. If the smoke test shows generationConfig.responseMimeType: "application/json" is accepted without a 400, keep it as a bonus; code must work without it.

Install

Endpoint

Fields
model, messages[{role: system|user|assistant|tool, content, images?: [base64]}], stream:false for one JSON object, format: "json" | <JSON schema> (structured outputs), options.temperature, options.num_ctx, keep_alive (default 5m), think (boolean or "low"/"medium"/"high"/"max" for thinking models).
Images
Raw base64 strings in messages[].images, no data:image/...;base64, prefix (the docs example is a bare PNG base64).
Response
message.content (string, JSON when format set), done, done_reason, total_duration (ns), prompt_eval_count, eval_count, eval_duration. Show total_duration/1e9 seconds in the UI as "on-device, 12.4 s".
Pre-warm
"If the messages array is empty, the model will be loaded into memory": curl localhost:11434/api/chat -d '{"model":"gemma4:e4b","messages":[],"keep_alive":"2h"}'. Run this as npm run warm before the demo. keep_alive: 0 unloads.

7. USDA: food safety after a power outage (citation) PAGES 403 TO CURL; RULE FROM USDA SEARCH SNIPPETS

Primary citation: USDA FSIS, Keep Your Food Safe During Emergencies: Power Outages, Floods & Fires. # Blackout Box

## When to use
Before a hurricane: build the pack. After: answer from the pack with no network.

## Steps
1. Pack (online): `node scripts/pack.mjs --lat 25.77 --lon -80.19 --meds "Lantus,Humalog,Trulicity,metformin" --out pack.json`
   Writes zone, places, fema, forecast, rules, law_252_358, fda_insulin, usda_outage with source + fetchedAt.
2. Countdowns (no model): `node scripts/engine.mjs --pack pack.json --outage "2026-10-10T04:00:00-04:00" --temp 84`
   Prints a Verdict[] for every item in the pack.
3. Ask (any model): `node scripts/ask.mjs --pack pack.json --lang ht "Ki kote mwen ka jwenn kouran?"`
   Builds the P2 GROUNDED_QA prompt from references/PROMPTS.md and sends it to OLLAMA_URL (default http://localhost:11434,
   model gemma4:e4b) or, if GEMINI_API_KEY is set and online, to the Gemini API gemma-4-26b-a4b-it.

## Safety rules (must follow)
- Numbers and deadlines come from engine.mjs, never from the model.
- Never tell the user to stop or discard medication unless status is DO_NOT_USE (frozen). Food past the USDA limit: say discard.
- If the pack does not contain the answer, say so. Do not guess shelters, roads, curfews, or coverage.
- Hazard photos (downed wires, water near electricity, gas smell): stay out, call 911 when service returns.
- End every answer with: "FDA, USDA, NWS, FEMA and Miami-Dade data. Not medical advice. In an emergency call 911."
