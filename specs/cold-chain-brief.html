Cold Chain

"The power's out. Is my insulin still good?"

Snap one photo of your meds. Cold Chain reads every label, pulls each drug's official storage limits from the FDA label, and turns them into a live countdown for each medication based on the heat in your home. It also checks recalls and shows the nearest places with power. Works in English, Español, and Kreyòl.

Demo moment: 4 bottles → 4 countdowns. Power out for 30 h.
Preview only. The numbers come from the engine in this brief: label room-temperature days × 0.9 safety margin, minus hours out of the fridge. The label limits are real openFDA text pulled today (Lantus, Humalog, Trulicity, metformin).
Part A

Concept: for judges and the team

The problem in Miami

Verified this morning: At 10:20 AM ET, Miami International (KMIA, NWS) read 31°C / 87.8°F with a heat index of 36.5°C / 97.7°F. That is already above the 86°F (30°C) room-temperature limit printed on every insulin and GLP-1 label we pulled today: Lantus, Humalog, Basaglar, and Trulicity. And this is an ordinary October morning with the power on.

Hurricanes take the fridge

After a storm, outages can last for days. Insulin and GLP-1 pens are meant to be refrigerated. The label gives each one a fixed number of days at room temperature, and only up to 86°F. In a closed Miami apartment with no AC, the room gets hotter than that.

Nobody knows the rules for their own meds

The limits differ by product: Lantus gets 28 days, Trulicity 14, and Humalog Mix KwikPens only 10. That information sits in section 16.2 of a package insert most people threw away. FDA's emergency guidance is detailed, but it is written for clinicians.

The people most at risk get served last

Seniors, people managing diabetes alone, and Spanish- and Kreyòl-speaking households need this answer in their own language, on a phone running on its last battery, without reading an FDA PDF.

A wrong answer either way is dangerous

Insulin cooked past its limit loses potency, so blood sugar runs high. But throwing insulin away with no replacement is worse. FDA says that in an emergency, insulin stored above 86°F may still need to be used. The tool has to be precise and careful about this.

Before you put Miami-Dade language or population figures in the pitch, pull them from Census ACS and cite them in the README. This brief does not assert any demographic numbers.

The four forces: Water (hurricane, flooding), Air and Fire (extreme heat), Land (where people shelter and where the power is). Cold Chain sits right where they meet for the thousands of households whose medicine depends on a working fridge.

How it works in 3 steps

01 · SNAP

One photo of all your meds

A vision model (Claude Sonnet 5.5 through the Cloudflare AI Gateway) reads every label in the photo: brand, generic, strength, and form (pen, vial, tablet). Anything it isn't sure about comes back as a field you edit. It never guesses.

02 · LOOK UP

Official FDA storage limits

openFDA drug labels return each drug's storage_and_handling text. A model turns that text into a rule (max °F, days at room temperature, do not freeze), and code then checks that every number appears in the quoted label text. Recalls are checked against openFDA enforcement data.

03 · COUNT DOWN

A live clock per medication

The heat in your home (a thermometer reading, or an estimate from live NWS data) plus the hours since the outage started give each medication a countdown and a status. It also shows the nearest library, fire station, or 24-hour EV charger where there is likely power, and your Florida early-refill right.

Fixed footer on every result screen, in all 3 languages, hard-coded (never generated by the model):

  • ES: Esta información proviene del etiquetado de medicamentos de la FDA. No es consejo médico. Llame a su farmacéutico o médico. En una emergencia, llame al 911.
  • HT: Enfòmasyon sa a soti nan etikèt medikaman FDA a. Se pa konsèy medikal. Rele famasyen oswa doktè ou. Si se yon ijans, rele 911. (Have a Kreyòl speaker at the event check this before the demo.)

Rules the product follows

  • Numbers (limits, clocks) come from label text plus deterministic code. The model only reads photos, extracts rules (which code then validates), and explains.
  • Every verdict links to its source: openFDA set_id, label effective_time, and the quoted sentence.
  • Never tells anyone to stop or throw out insulin without a replacement. This follows FDA emergency guidance: keep it cool, monitor blood sugar, replace it when you can. Frozen insulin is the one exception: "do not use", per FDA and the label.
  • If no storage text is found, the status is UNKNOWN with "ask your pharmacist". The app never guesses a limit.
  • Conservative by design: it uses 90% of the label's room-temperature allowance and treats anything above the label max as over the limit.

Privacy and honesty

  • The photo is processed in memory and never stored. There are no accounts and no tracking.
  • openFDA's own disclaimer (in every response) says "do not rely on openFDA to make decisions regarding medical care." Show it on the About screen and in the README.
  • The indoor-temperature estimate is labeled as an estimate, and the app asks for a thermometer reading.
  • "Places likely to have power" is a hint, not a promise. Show the data source and when it was checked.
Part B

Build intel: for the coding agent

Everything below was checked with real calls on Sat Oct 10, 2026, between 10:30 and 10:45 AM ET, unless marked otherwise. Raw responses are saved in reports/cold-chain-research/. Build time ends at 3:30 PM ET, and submission in OrganizerHQ is due by 3:45 PM.

Recommended stack

Vite + TypeScript front end, plus one Node 20 Express server. Why: a single process holds the keys server-side, caches every upstream response to disk for the demo fallback, and runs from a laptop with no deploy step. The core engine lives in the Agent Skill as dependency-free ESM, and the server imports it, so there is one source of truth.

Front end (web/)

  • Vite + vanilla TS, with no framework. Use mobile-first CSS that follows this brief's colors.
  • Camera: <input type="file" accept="image/*" capture="environment">. Resize on the client to a long edge of 1568 px, JPEG quality 0.85, then base64.
  • Countdowns: one setInterval(1000) that re-renders from hoursLeft and computedAt.
  • i18n: a static dictionary in web/src/i18n.ts for en, es, and ht.
  • Vite dev proxy: /api → http://localhost:8787.

Back end (server/)

  • Express on Node 20 via tsx, with express.json({limit:"12mb"}).
  • Native fetch, no SDKs. Read secrets from env only (see env vars).
  • Disk cache: cache/<sha1(url+body)>.json with a TTL (labels 24 h, weather 10 min, places 1 h). DEMO_MODE=1 means cache-only.
  • Production: npm run build && npm start. Express serves web/dist. Cloud Run is optional, after 3:30.
  • Tests: node --test on the engine. It needs no test-runner install and keeps machine load low.

Architecture and data flow

  1. The phone posts a resized photo to /api/scan.
  2. Sonnet 5.5 (vision, through the AI Gateway in Anthropic Messages format) returns JSON with each medication. The user confirms or edits anything with low confidence.
  3. For each medication, /api/label queries openFDA, picks the best label, and extracts a storage rule. Code validates that the numbers appear in the quote. /api/recalls runs in parallel.
  4. /api/weather gets the NWS observation now plus the hourly forecast, which feeds the indoor estimate. A thermometer reading from the user overrides it.
  5. /api/places merges Miami-Dade libraries, fire stations, and open shelters with NREL 24-hour EV chargers, sorted by distance.
  6. engine.mjs computes status and hoursLeft for each medication on the client and the server. It is the same file in both places, and it re-runs live when the slider moves.
  7. /api/explain has Fable 5.1 write a plain-language explanation in the chosen language. It cannot change status or numbers.

1. Cloudflare AI Gateway: vision and text VERIFIED 10:40 ET

Endpoint
POST https://api.cloudflare.com/client/v4/accounts/${CF_ACCOUNT_ID}/ai/v1/messages (Anthropic Messages format)
Auth
Authorization: Bearer ${CF_AIG_TOKEN} + cf-aig-gateway-id: default + anthropic-version: 2023-06-01 + content-type: application/json. On Micah's machine the token is at ~/.config/cloudflare/claude-api-token and the account ID at ~/.config/cloudflare/account-id. Load both into env and never commit or print them.
Models
anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5 (photo/label read, fast), anthropic/claude-fable-5.1 (rule extraction and explanations), anthropic/claude-opus-5.5 (spare). max_tokens is required. Keep it ≤ 8192 (the skill notes say a 16000 request hung on Opus).
Rate
The default gateway allows 50 requests/min and caches for 300 s (per Micah's gateway skill notes).

Gotcha (verified): sending the image through the OpenAI-compatible /ai/v1/chat/completions with an image_url data URI silently dropped the image. The response showed prompt_tokens: 106 and returned {"meds":[]}. The same image through /ai/v1/messages with an Anthropic image block worked (input_tokens: 339, correct read). Use /ai/v1/messages for every call.

Gotchas (all observed today)

  • Repackager labels come first and lack storage text. brand_name:"humalog" with no filter returned an A-S Medication Solutions repackager label (NDC 50090-…) whose how_supplied is just "3 mL in a SYRINGE / 5 in a CARTON". Fix: add +AND+_exists_:storage_and_handling, request limit=5, prefer results where manufacturer_name is not a repackager and the text contains "°F" or "Store".
  • "Ozempic" now matches oral tablets. brand_name:"ozempic" returned an OZEMPIC, RYBELSUS label with route ORAL (Ozempic 1.5 mg / 4 mg tablets). The injectable Ozempic only came back as an A-S Medication repackager label with no storage text, and generic_name:"semaglutide"+AND+route:"SUBCUTANEOUS"+AND+_exists_:storage_and_handling returned No matches. Use dosage_form and route from the photo to pick a route, and fall back to UNKNOWN.
  • Some brands have no label with a storage_and_handling field: victoza and synthroid with _exists_:storage_and_handling returned NOT_FOUND. Retry without the filter and parse how_supplied. If there is still no "Store…" sentence, the status is UNKNOWN.
  • Generic search matches combination products. The top metformin hit was sitagliptin+metformin. Use openfda.generic_name.exact:"METFORMIN HYDROCHLORIDE" for single-ingredient products (verified: 247 results). But the first exact hit was a repackager with no "Store" text, so the same fix applies.
  • One label can cover multiple products with different limits (Humalog vial vs Mix KwikPen 10 days). The rule extractor must pick the row that matches the device in the photo, or take the shortest limit if unsure.
  • No match returns {"error":{"code":"NOT_FOUND","message":"No matches found!"}} with HTTP 404. Treat it as an empty result, not a crash.
  • Every response's meta.disclaimer says: "Do not rely on openFDA to make decisions regarding medical care … assume all results are unvalidated." Show it.

7. Florida early-refill law (content, not an API) VERIFIED TEXT

Fla. Stat. § 252.358, "Emergency-preparedness prescription medication refills." Insurers, HMOs, and other OIR-licensed entities that cover prescriptions must waive refill time restrictions, including "refill too soon" edits, and pay for at least a 30-day supply, when the insured lives in a county that (a) is under an NWS hurricane warning, (b) is under a Governor-declared state of emergency, or (c) has activated its emergency operations center and plan. It applies to refills requested within 30 days of the trigger, or until the conditions end, and OIR can extend it in 15- or 30-day increments. The insured must still have refills remaining. 8. FDA emergency insulin guidance (content for the explain step) VERIFIED TEXT

Source: FDA, "Information Regarding Insulin Storage and Switching Between Products in an Emergency" (page says content current as of 09/28/2026). Key rules, paraphrased:

  • Manufacturer vials or cartridges, opened or unopened, may be left unrefrigerated at 59°F–86°F for up to 28 days.
  • In an emergency, insulin stored above 86°F may still need to be used. Keep it as cool as possible and away from direct heat and sunlight.
  • If you use ice, don't freeze the insulin. Do not use insulin that has been frozen.
  • Throw away pump insulin exposed to temperatures above 98.6°F. Replace pump infusion sets after 48 h unless the device instructions say otherwise.
  • Replace exposed insulin once proper storage is available again.
  • Switching products should happen under a doctor's care. The page gives unit-for-unit swap rules. Do not put dosing conversions in the app. Link to the FDA page and tell the user to call a clinician.

Verdict logic (deterministic, conservative)

The engine is pure JS with no I/O. It lives at skills/cold-chain-check/scripts/engine.mjs and is imported by the server, the web app, and the skill CLI. The model never computes a number.

Statuses

  • OK under the label max, more than 48 h of room-temperature allowance left
  • USE_SOON 48 h or less left, or forecast to pass the max within the window
  • OVER_LIMIT indoor temp is above the label max, or the room-temperature days are used up. Copy: "Past the label limit. Don't stop your insulin without a replacement. Keep it cool, check your sugar, get a replacement."
  • DO_NOT_USE only when the medication was frozen and the label says do not freeze (FDA + label)
  • UNKNOWN no storage text found. "Ask your pharmacist."

Conservative choices (ours, not FDA's; state them in the README)

  • Use 90% of the label's room-temperature allowance (SAFETY_MARGIN = 0.9).
  • If one label covers several products, use the shortest limit unless the device is confirmed.
  • Count every hour since the outage started as room-temperature time, even if the fridge stayed cold for a while.
  • Indoor estimate (our heuristic, not validated): after 4 h without power, indoor = max(80°F, outdoor now, max of the next 6 hourly forecast temps). Always ask for a thermometer reading.
// skills/cold-chain-check/scripts/engine.mjs
/** @typedef {{kind:"refrigerated"|"room_temp"|"unknown", maxTempF:number|null, roomTempDays:number|null,
 *   inUseDays:number|null, doNotFreeze:boolean, quote:string,
 *   source:{setId:string, effectiveTime:string, field:string}}} Rule
 *  @typedef {{indoorTempF:number, indoorSource:"user"|"estimate", hoursOutOfFridge:number,
 *   openedDaysAgo?:number, frozen?:boolean, forecastIndoorF?:{time:string,tempF:number}[]}} Exposure */
export const SAFETY_MARGIN = 0.9;
export const USE_SOON_HOURS = 48;

export function verdict(rule, x, now = Date.now()) {
  if (!rule || rule.kind === "unknown" || rule.maxTempF == null)
    return { status: "UNKNOWN", hoursLeft: null, reasons: ["no_label_limits"] };
  if (x.frozen && rule.doNotFreeze)
    return { status: "DO_NOT_USE", hoursLeft: 0, reasons: ["frozen"] };
  if (x.indoorTempF > rule.maxTempF)
    return { status: "OVER_LIMIT", hoursLeft: 0, reasons: ["temp_above_label_max"] };

  let hoursLeft = null;
  if (rule.kind === "refrigerated" && rule.roomTempDays != null) {
    const budgets = [rule.roomTempDays * 24 * SAFETY_MARGIN - x.hoursOutOfFridge];
    if (x.openedDaysAgo != null && rule.inUseDays != null)
      budgets.push(rule.inUseDays * 24 * SAFETY_MARGIN - x.openedDaysAgo * 24);
    hoursLeft = Math.min(...budgets);
    if (hoursLeft <= 0) return { status: "OVER_LIMIT", hoursLeft: 0, reasons: ["room_temp_days_used_up"] };
  }

  // forecast-aware: when does the room cross the label max?
  const cross = (x.forecastIndoorF || []).find(p => p.tempF > rule.maxTempF);
  const hoursToCross = cross ? Math.max(0, (Date.parse(cross.time) - now) / 36e5) : null;

  const soon = (hoursLeft != null && hoursLeft < USE_SOON_HOURS) || (hoursToCross != null && hoursToCross < USE_SOON_HOURS);
  return { status: soon ? "USE_SOON" : "OK", hoursLeft, hoursToCross, crossAt: cross?.time ?? null,
           reasons: soon ? ["low_allowance_or_heat_coming"] : [] };
}

export function estimateIndoorF(outdoorNowF, hourlyF, hoursSinceOutage) {
  if (hoursSinceOutage < 4) return Math.max(78, outdoorNowF - 4);           // heuristic, label as estimate
  return Math.max(80, outdoorNowF, ...hourlyF.slice(0, 6));
}

# Cold Chain check

## When to use
A user has meds that need refrigeration, the power is out or it is hot, and they ask if the meds are still OK.

## Steps
1. Get each drug's brand or generic name, device (pen/vial/tablet), whether it is opened (and how many days ago),
   hours since refrigeration was lost, and the indoor temperature in °F (ask for a thermometer reading).
2. Run: `node scripts/check.mjs --drug "Lantus" --device pen --hours 30 --temp 84 [--opened-days 20] [--frozen]`
3. The script prints JSON: {drug, rule{maxTempF, roomTempDays, quote, source}, status, hoursLeft, recalls[]}.
4. Report status and hours left, and quote the label sentence and its source (openFDA set_id).

## Safety rules (must follow)
- Never tell the user to stop or discard insulin unless status is DO_NOT_USE (frozen).
- For OVER_LIMIT: keep it cool, check blood sugar more often, get a replacement.
  In Florida, F.S. 252.358 allows early refills during hurricane warnings or declared emergencies.
- If status is UNKNOWN, say the label limits were not found and to ask a pharmacist.
- Always end with: "FDA label information, not medical advice. Call your pharmacist or doctor; in an emergency call 911."

## Details
See [references/RULES.md](references/RULES.md) for the engine math and [references/SOURCES.md](references/SOURCES.md).
  • scripts/check.mjs uses only Node built-ins and fetch. It queries openFDA, extracts the rule with a regex-first parser (patterns like up to (\d+)°F and total of (\d+) days), and uses an LLM only if the regex fails and CF_AIG_TOKEN is set. That keeps the skill runnable with no proprietary model, which strengthens the open-source case.
  • scripts/engine.mjs is the same file the app imports. Say so in the README: "the app is a UI over this open skill".
